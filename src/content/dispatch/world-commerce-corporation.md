# The World Commerce Corporation: OSS, Intrepid, and the Postwar Shadow Market

## What Was It?

The **World Commerce Corporation (WCC)** was a private international trading company formed in the aftermath of World War II. Founded in **Panama in 1945**, it became active in September 1947 with roughly **$1 million in working capital** pledged by its principals and a small circle of investors. It described itself as a vehicle for world trade — especially trade with developing nations that were hamstrung by postwar currency restrictions.

**Key proven fact:** The WCC was originally registered as the **British American Canadian Corporation (BACC)**. In 1947, BACC was formally absorbed into the World Commerce Corporation. This is *not* a central bank from England, as is sometimes assumed; it was a private commercial corporation whose leadership was drawn heavily from wartime intelligence and banking circles.

## The Intelligence Roots

The WCC was the brainchild of two legendary figures of Allied intelligence:

- **William J. "Wild Bill" Donovan** — founder and chief of the U.S. **Office of Strategic Services (OSS)**, the direct predecessor of the CIA.
- **Sir William Stephenson** — head of **British Security Coordination (BSC)** in New York, known by his cover name "Intrepid," and widely regarded as one of the architects of modern Anglo-American intelligence cooperation.

William Donovan is reported to have described the organization as a **"commercially-oriented espionage net."** That phrase alone captures the ambiguity that defines the WCC: a private company trading in goods and services, but built by spies.

## The Leadership Roster

According to archived accounts and secondary research, the WCC leadership included:

- **Frank Ryan** — OSS veteran, later WCC president; a former partner in **Jules Bache & Co.**
- **William Horrigan** — OSS, listed as WCC president
- **Ricardo Sicre** — OSS, listed as vice-president
- **Russell Forgan** — OSS, listed as director
- **James Cavagnaro** — vice-president of Transamerica (the holding company that controlled Bank of America)
- **Daniel de Menocal** — investment banker connected to **J.P. Morgan**
- **L. Boyd Hatch** — investment banker
- **Edward Stettinius Jr.** — former U.S. Secretary of State, organizer of the Liberia Company
- **William Stephenson** — BSC/Intrepid, listed as a director
- **John Arthur Reed Pepper** — associated with BSC, listed as vice-president

Some sources also connect **Nelson Rockefeller** and **David Ogilvy** (the future advertising titan who had worked for BSC) to the WCC orbit. In his autobiography, Ogilvy describes being a frequent lunch partner of **Samuel Bronfman**, the Canadian distiller and financier.

**Provenance note:** The leadership list is well-attested across multiple independent sources. The precise depth of involvement of figures such as Rockefeller is less certain and should be treated as reported, not proven.

## Business Model: "Invisible Cartel"

The WCC specialized in trade deals that bypassed normal currency controls by using **barter arrangements and dollar guarantees**. Former employee Tom Hill recalled that the idea was to leverage wartime intelligence networks and international contacts to set up companies, mostly in Central and South America.

Reported WCC ventures span an unusually broad range of industries and geographies:

- Oil deals in the Gulf of Mexico
- Asbestos mining in Venezuela
- Tourist development in Spain
- Chili pepper and soybean trading
- Sale of surplus military aircraft to the **Kuomintang (KMT)** in China
- Development of the postwar **Thai silk trade**
- The **Caribbean Cement Company** in Jamaica, where Stephenson became chairman and which was profitable by the early 1960s

One account suggests the WCC was designed to **fill the void left by the break-up of the big German cartels**, which Stephenson's wartime operations had helped destroy. Whether that was the strategic intent or merely a useful side-effect remains debated.

## Controversies and Shadow Connections

### 1. The "Little ECA"

A 1949 *New York Times* article nicknamed the WCC a **"Little ECA"** — an informal, private-sector counterpart to the Marshall Plan's Economic Cooperation Administration. The framing was provocative: a private company run by former spies acting in a space normally occupied by governments and aid institutions.

### 2. Cold War Dealings

A declassified State Department cable from the early 1950s records WCC president Frank Ryan negotiating on behalf of a German steel company to purchase a Czechoslovak strip mill held under a U.S. Treasury blocking order. The proposed transaction required U.S. government approval and Treasury licensing — clear evidence that WCC operations intersected with high-stakes Cold War trade policy, even if the transaction itself may not have been illicit.

### 3. The FBI Question

Rumors persist that the **FBI opened or considered opening an investigation of the WCC** and that the inquiry was stifled because of the company's powerful intelligence and banking connections. To date, I have not located a declassified FBI file or uncontested primary document confirming this specific claim. The WCC's activities were clearly visible to U.S. regulators (the Treasury licensing case above is proof), but whether there was a formal, suppressed FBI investigation is **speculative and unverified**.

### 4. Permindex and the JFK Assassination Fringe

The WCC name appears in the conspiracy literature around the **John F. Kennedy assassination**, primarily through **William Torbitt's** manuscript *Nomenclature of an Assassination Cabal* (1970). Torbitt linked elements of the WCC/BSC network to **Permindex**, a Swiss-based trade organization, and to **Clay Shaw's** Centro Mondiale Commerciale in Italy. Torbitt alleged that these entities were fronts for assassination planning.

**This material is highly speculative and disputed.** Mainstream historians and journalists — including evidence from the Mitrokhin archives — have traced some of the original Permindex allegations to **KGB disinformation** planted in the Italian press in the late 1960s. Torbitt's claims should be read as part of the assassination-conspiracy archive, not as proven history.

## Verdict

The World Commerce Corporation was a real and active company in the 1945-1950s global economy. Its leadership was overwhelmingly composed of former **OSS and BSC** officers, allied with banking, industrial, and political elites. It operated across multiple continents and sectors using financial structures that blurred the line between private commerce and statecraft.

What is **documented**:
- Its founding by Donovan and Stephenson
- Its origins as BACC
- Its intelligence-veteran leadership
- Its broad global trading activities
- Its intersection with official Cold War trade controls

What is **speculative, conjectural, or disputed**:
- Whether it was deliberately designed as a global cartel-replacement scheme
- Claims of a suppressed FBI investigation
- Any connection to the JFK assassination through Permindex

The WCC is best understood as a case study in how wartime intelligence networks were converted into postwar economic power — and how the resulting opacity naturally invites both legitimate suspicion and unfounded conspiracy theory.
