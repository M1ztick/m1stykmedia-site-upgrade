# **DISPATCH**

## **Trump Untruth Tracker: The Second Term Ledger**

*A survey of Donald Trump’s false and misleading public statements from the 2024 campaign through August 2026*

---

### **I. The Short Version**

The most rigorous count of Donald Trump’s falsehoods remains the **Washington Post Fact Checker’s tally of 30,573 false or misleading claims during his first term**. No equivalent live national database exists for his second term, but major fact-checkers — CNN, the Post, PolitiFact, AP, and Reuters — have continued documenting a high volume of untruths across speeches, interviews, social media, and formal addresses.

What we can say with confidence:

- **First term:** 30,573 false or misleading claims (about 21 per day).
- **2024 campaign:** Fact-checkers logged a surge in false claims, and PolitiFact named Trump its **2024 Lie of the Year**.
- **Second term, first 100 days:** CNN identified **100 separate false claims** in a single article debunking his first 100 days.
- **Second term, one-year mark:** CNN published a dedicated fact-check of false claims on **January 20, 2026**.
- **Current status (August 2026):** Multiple fact-checks per week continue; outlets are tracking major false claims, but **no authoritative cumulative counter exists** for the second term.

A viral social-media claim alleging **85,654 false statements by July 2026** has circulated widely, but that number is **not confirmed by any recognized fact-checking database** and should be treated as unverified.

---

### **II. The Gold Standard: First-Term 30,573**

The *Washington Post*’s Fact Checker project, maintained by Glenn Kessler, produced the most cited presidential dishonesty tally in modern history. The project tracked every verified false or misleading statement across Trump’s presidency:

| Period | Total false/misleading claims |
|---|---|
| Jan. 20, 2017 – Jan. 10, 2021 | **30,526** |
| Jan. 20, 2017 – end of term (Jan. 20, 2021) | **30,573** |
| Average per day | ~21 |

The database was not restarted as a public, continuously updating second-term project, though the Post’s Fact Checker column has continued publishing Trump fact-checks.

---

### **III. The 2024 Campaign: Lie of the Year**

Before returning to office, Trump ran a 2024 campaign that fact-checkers described as saturated with falsehoods:

- **CNN’s Daniel Dale** reported that Trump was making dozens of false or misleading claims per rally and per interview — rates often higher than in 2016 or 2020.
- **PolitiFact** named Donald Trump its **2024 Lie of the Year**, citing repeated false claims about immigration, crime, the economy, and election denial.
- **The Washington Post** also resumed tracking campaign-trail claims and found a sharp surge compared with previous cycles.

No single authoritative full-year total for 2024 has been published, but the pattern was consistent with the first term’s pace or faster.

---

### **IV. Second Term: First 100 Days**

On **April 30, 2025**, CNN published a comprehensive debunking article titled **“Fact check: Debunking 100 Trump false claims from his first 100 days.”**

That article documented **100 separate false claims** in just over three months, covering:

- Immigration and border claims
- Economic and inflation claims
- Foreign policy assertions
- Election-related statements
- Energy and climate remarks
- Figures about federal programs

This gives a second-term baseline of **roughly 1 false claim per day** at minimum from CNN alone, though the actual volume was almost certainly higher because fact-checkers cannot review every utterance.

---

Major fact-checking attention continued into 2026:

| Feb. 24, 2026 | CNN / PBS / AP | Fact-check of Trump’s 2026 State of the Union address |

PolitiFact also declared **2025 the “Year of the Lies,”** noting that the volume of significant false statements by the Trump administration and other powerholders made it impossible to choose a single “Lie of the Year.”

---

### **VI. What About Press Interviews vs. Public Hearings?**

This is where the trail goes cold.

**Press interviews:** Interview appearances have been a major source of Trump false claims in every tracked period. The Post’s first-term venue analysis showed that, during the first 100 days of his first term, **press interviews were the second-largest category** of false/misleading claims after general remarks. No outlet has published an interview-only running count for the second term.

**Public hearings:** Sitting presidents rarely testify publicly before Congress. Trump has **not appeared at a congressional hearing** during his second term. Fact-checks of sworn testimony from earlier periods — for example, written responses to Robert Mueller, New York civil case depositions, and post-presidency court proceedings — are handled case-by-case, not as part of a running public-presidency tally.

So while we know interviews produce many falsehoods and hearings produce essentially none in this period, there is no separate authoritative count for either venue.

---

### **VII. The 85,654 Number: Treat It as Unverified**

A social-media meme has claimed that, as of **July 27, 2026**, Trump had made **85,654 false statements** across his presidencies, averaging about 169 per day.

This figure has **not been confirmed by the Washington Post, CNN, PolitiFact, AP, Reuters, or any other recognized fact-checking organization**.

The likely source appears to be an extrapolation or social-media estimate based on the first-term 30,573 count, not an independently verified count. It should not be cited as a hard fact.

---

### **VIII. Why There Is No Simple Second-Term Number**

Several factors prevent a clean cumulative count:

1. **The Post database was first-term only.** Its public database stopped on January 20, 2021.
2. **Different fact-checkers use different scopes.** PolitiFact rates specific statements; CNN counts per-article claims; the Post counted claims differently.
3. **Volume outpaces capacity.** With Trump producing many statements daily, no outlet has publicly committed to the same exhaustive daily transcription the Post performed from 2017–2021.
4. **Social-platform changes.** Truth Social, X (formerly Twitter), podcasts, and livestreams complicate real-time tracking compared with the structured first-term environment.

---

### **IX. Looking Forward**

If a second-term equivalent to the 30,573 figure ever materializes, it will almost certainly come from the same institutions that produced the first one. Until then, the safest statement is:

> Donald Trump’s rate of false or misleading public statements has remained extremely high during his second term, with fact-checkers documenting dozens to hundreds of major falsehoods per quarter, but **no authoritative cumulative second-term total exists** as of August 2026.
