# The Iran War Profit Sheet
## Who Makes Money When Missiles Fly

On February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched joint strikes against Iranian nuclear and military facilities, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and triggering a regional war that has lasted nearly four months. The human toll is measured in thousands dead, millions displaced, and a region pushed to the brink of wider conflagration.

The financial toll, however, looks very different depending on which ledger you read.

While civilians in Tehran, Beirut, and Gaza count their dead and wounded, a small cluster of corporations has logged what may be the most profitable quarter in the history of modern warfare. This is not a secret conspiracy. It is public record, filed in earnings statements, stock tickers, and procurement disclosures. The only thing hidden is the connection between the two ledgers — the one measured in lives and the one measured in dollars.

The broader S&P 500 fell. Defense rose. CNBC reported that defense was "a rare bright spot amid a broader market sell-off triggered by fears of a wider regional conflict."

The fears were justified. The profits were immediate.

## The Patriot Monopoly: A $3.7 Million Bullet

RTX Corporation — the parent company of Raytheon — is the single largest corporate beneficiary of the Iran war for one simple reason: it is the **sole manufacturer** of the Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptor, the backbone of Saudi, American, and allied air defense.

Each interceptor costs approximately **$3.7 million**.

In the first 11 days of war, Saudi Arabia fired an estimated **300 to 450** Patriot interceptors. The cost: **$1.1 to $1.67 billion** in ammunition alone. The United States, defending its own bases and Saudi infrastructure, burned through an additional **$2.4 billion** worth of interceptors in just five days.

This is ammunition expenditure only. It does not include radar systems, fire control units, command posts, logistics chains, or the personnel operating them. Every dollar flows to RTX and its subcontractors.

And there is no alternative. Saudi Arabia cannot switch suppliers mid-war. The Patriot system is proprietary. The interceptors are single-source. The dependency is structural, intentional, and enormously profitable.

## The Cost Asymmetry: Why Iran Wins by Losing

The most economically consequential fact of the Iran war is the cost ratio between attack and defense:

| Iranian Weapon | Estimated Cost | Defender's Response | Cost | Ratio |
|---------------|---------------|---------------------|------|-------|
| Shahed-136 drone | $20,000–$80,000 | Patriot PAC-3 MSE | $3,700,000 | **46:1 to 185:1** |
| Emad/Ghadr ballistic missile | $500,000–$2M | PAC-3 + THAAD backup | $16,000,000–$19M | **8:1 to 38:1** |

Iran does not need to penetrate Saudi or American air defenses to succeed strategically. It only needs to force the defender to keep firing interceptors at a rate that depletes stockpiles faster than production lines can replace them.

For RTX, Lockheed Martin, and their subcontractors, this equation is not a problem. It is the business model. Every Iranian drone triggers a replacement order. Every replacement order has a multi-year production timeline. Every production timeline carries margins that would make most industries envious.

The war does not need to be won to be profitable. It only needs to continue.

## The Backlog Archipelago

Defense contractors measure wealth not in quarterly earnings but in **backlogs** — the value of orders already booked but not yet delivered. The Iran war has pushed these numbers into the stratosphere:

| Company | Pre-War Backlog/Performance | Post-War Trajectory |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **RTX Corporation** | $268 billion total backlog; $107B defense orders | "Almost certainly conservative" now |
| **Lockheed Martin** | $68.4 billion 2024 revenue | Accelerated PAC-3 production agreement |
| **Northrop Grumman** | +60% over 3 years | Continued surge |
| **Elbit Systems** (Israel) | +45% year-to-date | Iron Dome, precision munitions export boom |

The $9 billion Saudi Patriot deal announced in January 2026 — before the war began — now looks like a down payment. Replacement orders, accelerated procurement, and emergency spending authority have turned the defense sector into a seller's market with no ceiling in sight.

## The Oil Variable: BP, Exxon, and the Hormuz Premium

The war's closure of the Strait of Hormuz — through which one-fifth of global oil flows — sent prices soaring more than 50%. The oil sector's windfall is real but uneven:

| Company | Q1 2026 Performance | Notes |
|---------|---------------------|-------|
| **BP** | **Profits more than doubled** | Called performance "exceptional" |
| **TotalEnergies** | **+30% jump** | Share buybacks and increased dividends |
| **ConocoPhillips** | **+84%** ($2.3B profit) | Pre-war comparison |
| **Valero Energy** | $1.2B profit | Refining margins surged |
| **ExxonMobil** | **-45%** ($4.2B vs $7.7B) | Delivery disruptions; hedge losses |
| **Chevron** | **-33%** | Strait dependency |

Exxon and Chevron suffered short-term because their physical production and delivery chains depend on Hormuz transit. But analysts predict rapid recovery: if higher prices hold, Exxon's Q2 earnings may **more than double** year-over-year, and Chevron's full-year profits are predicted to rise **56%**.

The disruption is temporary. The price premium is structural. The net transfer is from consumers at the pump to shareholders at the boardroom.

## The Finance Layer: BlackRock and the Shareholder Class

Between 2020 and 2025, top military contractors spent **$110 billion on stock buybacks and dividends** — more than double their capital expenditures. This wealth does not flow to workers or to reinvestment. It flows to shareholders.

The top 1% of earners control roughly half of all stock market wealth in the United States. This means the primary beneficiaries of defense contractor profits — generated by taxpayer-funded arms sales, emergency procurement, and foreign military financing — are the wealthiest households in America.

BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, holds billions in RTX, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, and their peers. The Iran war has increased the value of these holdings substantially.

The cycle is nearly closed: taxpayer money → Pentagon procurement → defense contractor revenue → shareholder buybacks → concentrated wealth → political donations → friendly legislators → more Pentagon procurement.

## The Pentagon Windfall

The war unlocked spending authority that would have taken years to secure in peacetime:

| Funding Item | Amount |
|-------------|--------|
| Extra Pentagon money under consideration (pre-war) | $500 billion |
| Additional military funding announced (post-strike) | **$153 billion** |
| Expected timeline for $153B | Originally 5 years; now compressed |

This is not supplemental appropriation for a defined mission. This is **structural budget acceleration** — taking multi-year procurement cycles and compressing them into months, bypassing normal oversight and competitive bidding constraints.

## The Managed Instability Thesis

The Iran war does not need to end to serve its economic function. Managed instability — enough conflict to justify massive procurement, enough ceasefire to prevent market panic — is the optimal condition for the permanent war economy.

The June 14 "peace deal" fits this pattern precisely. It is a US-Iran bilateral arrangement that explicitly excludes Israel, which immediately rejects its key provisions and continues bombing Lebanon. The deal will likely collapse. The weapons orders will remain. The stock prices will stay elevated. And the next announcement, six or twelve months from now, will follow the same script.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a system with predictable incentives. The defense industry makes money from tension, not resolution. The oil industry makes money from scarcity, not abundance. The financial industry makes money from volatility, not stability. And the political system makes money from all three.

The only thing not making money is peace.
