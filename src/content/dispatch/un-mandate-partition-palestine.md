# From Balfour to Nakba: How Palestine Was Stolen by Decree and by Force

## I. A Colonial Frame from the Start

Before the British arrived, Palestine had been part of the Ottoman Empire for roughly 400 years. It was not empty. It was not waiting. Its people—Arab Muslims, Arab Christians, and a small indigenous Jewish minority—lived in cities and villages across a functioning society.

During World War I, Britain made three promises that it never intended to keep simultaneously:

- **To the Arabs** (Hussein–McMahon correspondence, 1915–16): support for an independent Arab kingdom if they rebelled against the Ottomans.
- **To the French** (Sykes–Picot Agreement, 1916): a secret carve-up of the Middle East into colonial spheres.
- **To the Zionists** (Balfour Declaration, 1917): support for "the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people."

There is no honest reading of these three commitments that makes them compatible. Palestine was promised to its Arab inhabitants, then promised again to European Zionists, while Britain and France divided the region between themselves.

## II. The Balfour Declaration: A Land Theft in Polite Language

The Balfour Declaration of November 2, 1917, is often treated with reverence in Western discourse as a noble gesture toward Jewish self-determination. But the document itself is an act of imperial arrogance: a British foreign minister promising a piece of someone else's country to a European political movement.

Its language contains a transparent contradiction. It supports a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine while claiming it should not prejudice the "civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities." The phrase "non-Jewish communities" is itself erasure: the Palestinians were not a footnote or a minority. They were roughly 90% of the population.

This contradiction was never resolved. It was simply enforced. The League of Nations **Mandate for Palestine (1923)** gave Britain legal cover to administer the territory while actively facilitating Jewish immigration, land acquisition, and state-building. The machinery of colonial law was put in service of demographic transformation.

## III. The Settler Project

Beginning in the late 19th century, and accelerating after 1905 and again after World War I, organized Jewish immigration to Palestine increased. By 1947, the Jewish population had grown from roughly 56,000 (under 10%) in 1918 to roughly 600,000 (about 33%).

Many immigrants were refugees from Russian pogroms and European antisemitism, and their motives were human and understandable. But their arrival was channeled into a political project: the creation of a Jewish-majority state in a land that was not Jewish-majority and had not been for many centuries.

The engine of this transformation was land. Zionist agencies purchased land, often from absentee Ottoman or Arab landlords, and evicted Arab tenant farmers who had worked it for generations. This was legal under colonial property law. It was also socially catastrophic. Entire villages lost their livelihoods to make way for kibbutzim and Jewish-only labor settlements.

The Zionist labor movement consciously pursued a policy of **Hebrew labor**—hiring Jews only—which deepened the economic separation between the two communities. The goal was not coexistence. It was a separate, self-sufficient Jewish society that would eventually become a Jewish state.

## IV. The British Get Bitten

Britain created the political conditions for Zionist expansion, then tried to slow it when Arab revolt and regional pressure made Jewish immigration inconvenient. The **1939 White Paper** restricted Jewish immigration and proposed a binational Palestine within ten years.

Zionist militias that had grown under British protection now turned on their sponsor.

- The **Haganah**, linked to the Jewish Agency, was the largest militia and eventually coordinated attacks with the others.
- The **Irgun**, led by Menachem Begin, carried out terror bombings, market massacres, and attacks on British personnel. In 1948, Albert Einstein, Hannah Arendt, and other Jewish intellectuals wrote to the *New York Times* condemning Begin's party as "a terrorist, right-wing chauvinist organization in Palestine, closely akin in its organization, methods, political philosophy and social appeal to the Nazi and Fascist parties."
- **Lehi** (the Stern Gang), led by Yitzhak Shamir, assassinated British officials and at one point sought support from Fascist Italy and Nazi Germany against Britain.

On **July 22, 1946**, Irgun operatives bombed the **King David Hotel** in Jerusalem, the headquarters of British colonial administration. Ninety-one people were killed: 41 Arabs, 28 Britons, 17 Jews, and 5 others. A plaque at the site today treats the bombers as freedom fighters. Many historians classify the attack as terrorism.

The Haganah, Irgun, and Lehi would later form the core of the **Israel Defense Forces**. The men who planted bombs in markets and hotels became prime ministers and generals.

## V. The U.N. Vote: Legitimacy for the Illegitimate

By 1947, Britain was exhausted and wanted out. It referred the matter to the United Nations. The U.N. Special Committee on Palestine recommended partition.

On **November 29, 1947**, the General Assembly adopted **Resolution 181**, calling for:

- A **Jewish state** on about 56% of the land
- An **Arab state** on about 42% of the land
- An **international zone** around Jerusalem and Bethlehem

This was sold as a compromise. It was not. Jews made up roughly 30–33% of the population and owned about 6–7% of the land. The proposed Jewish state was to receive 56% of the territory, and within it nearly half the population would be Arab. The Arab state, by contrast, would be fragmented and economically weaker.

The resolution passed 33–13 with 10 abstentions. The voting reflected Cold War lobbying, Western guilt over the Holocaust, and Palestine's absence from the deliberations. Palestinian representatives were not given a seat at the table.

Zionist leaders accepted the partition as a tactical victory, not a final border. Arab leaders rejected it as the imposition of a settler state on a colonized population. Both sides were right, in their own reading of what partition meant. Only one had the military capacity to reshape the outcome.

## VI. From Partition to Conquest

Violence erupted within hours of the vote. What began as intercommunal clashes quickly became a military campaign to expand beyond the U.N. lines.

Between November 1947 and July 1949, Zionist and then Israeli forces went on the offensive. They seized territory beyond what the U.N. had allotted. They destroyed or depopulated hundreds of Palestinian villages. They carried out massacres, including at **Deir Yassin** in April 1948, where over 100 Palestinian civilians were killed by Irgun and Lehi fighters.

The massacre was intentionally publicized to terrify surrounding populations into flight. It worked.

By the end of the 1948 war, Israel controlled roughly **78% of Mandatory Palestine**—far more than the 56% the U.N. had approved. Approximately **750,000 Palestinians were expelled or fled**. They took with them keys and deeds to homes they were never allowed to return to.

This is the **Nakba**: not a wartime exigency, but a systematic expulsion enabled by years of preparation, superior organization, and international indifference.

## VII. The Aftermath We Still Live In

The Palestinians who remained inside the new Israel became second-class citizens under military rule until 1966. The Palestinians who fled became refugees in Gaza, the West Bank, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria. Their descendants remain stateless or displaced.

Israel's 1950 **Law of Return** grants Jews anywhere in the world the right to immigrate and gain citizenship. Palestinian refugees with documented title to property inside Israel are denied the same right. The logic is unmistakable: the land belongs to Jews because the state says so.

Every settlement built in the West Bank and East Jerusalem since 1967, every demolition of a Palestinian home, every extension of the separation wall, every "Judaization" campaign inside Israel itself—these are not aberrations. They are continuations of the same project begun under the British Mandate: the replacement of a Palestinian-majority land with a Jewish-majority state by force, law, and demographic engineering.

## VIII. Conclusion

The partition of Palestine was not a fair two-state solution. It was an international rubber stamp on a settler-colonial project already underway. The British laid the foundation, the U.N. provided legitimacy, and Zionist militias completed the conquest.

From the Irgun's hotel bombing to the massacres of 1948, from the exclusion of Palestinians at the U.N. to the permanent displacement of refugees today, the violence was present at the beginning. It did not emerge later. It was the foundation.

Understanding this history is not a matter of ancient grievance. It is a matter of recognizing what is still being enforced at checkpoints, in courts, and on the ground every day.
