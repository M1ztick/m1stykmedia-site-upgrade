# **DISPATCH**

## **Iran Strikes Tankers, US Retaliates: The MOU Unravels During Khamenei's Funeral**

*Iran fired missiles at three commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. The US responded with airstrikes on Iranian military targets—all while Ayatollah Khamenei's body lay in state. Here's what actually happened, what the MOU said, and why both sides claim the other broke the deal first.*

---

### **I. The Attacks: What Happened (July 6–7, 2026)**

Over a roughly 24-hour period, **Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) attacked three commercial vessels** transiting through the Strait of Hormuz. This was not ambiguous, not accidental, and not deniable.

| Time | Target | Damage |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Monday night, July 6** | **Qatari LNG tanker *Al Rekayyat*** | Missile strike to port side, engine room. Significant damage, no casualties. Owned by Nakilat, Qatar's state LNG shipping arm. |
| **Monday night, July 6** | **Saudi crude tanker *Wedyan*** | IRGC missiles. Confirmed by Saudi foreign ministry. |
| **Tuesday morning, July 7** | **Third unidentified tanker** | Drone strike. Confirmed by Institute for the Study of War. |

The **UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO)** confirmed a tanker was hit by an "unknown projectile" roughly 8 nautical miles off Oman's coast, causing a fire. **Axios**, citing U.S. officials, reported Iran fired **at least two missiles** at commercial ships. Two vessels suffered **"significant damage."**

Qatar's foreign ministry spokesperson, **Majed Al-Ansari**, called the *Al Rekayyat* attack **"unacceptable,"** a **"clear violation of international law,"** and held Iran **"fully legally responsible"** for any resulting damage.

---

### **II. The Trigger: Who Decides Which Route Ships Take?**

The immediate cause was a dispute over **transit corridors** through the Strait of Hormuz.

After the June 15/17 MOU was signed, Iran began insisting that commercial vessels use **Iranian-designated routes** rather than the standard international shipping lanes near the Omani coast. The vessels Iran attacked were traveling via the **Omani coastal route**—the conventional, internationally recognized pathway coordinated with the Sultanate of Oman.

According to the **Institute for the Study of War**, Iran explicitly stated the attacks were retaliatory: **both vessels had failed to use Iranian-approved transit mechanisms** and instead followed routes "set by the United Nations in coordination with the Omani government." In the days prior, the IRGC had warned ships to remain on **IRGC-designated routes** or face consequences.

The underlying issue: **Iran is attempting to stop Western and Gulf efforts to bypass Iranian control of the strait.** Gulf states and the U.S. have been routing traffic along the Omani coast, effectively circumventing Iran's ability to use Hormuz as leverage. Iran's missile strikes were a violent assertion that **it, not Oman or the UN, controls the waterway.**

---

### **III. The MOU: What Articles 4 and 5 Actually Say**

The **Memorandum of Understanding** signed June 15 (announced) / June 17 (formally signed) is a 14-point document. These are the sections in dispute:

#### **Article 4 — U.S. Obligations:**
> *"Immediately upon the signing of this MOU, the United States of America will begin the removal of its naval blockade and any disturbances or impediments against the Islamic Republic of Iran, and it will fully end the naval blockade within 30 days. During this period, the traffic of vessels will be in proportion to the numbers of pre-war traffic being restored by the Islamic Republic of Iran. United States of America further undertakes to remove its forces from the proximity of the Islamic Republic of Iran within 30 days after the final deal."*

#### **Article 5 — Iran's Obligations:**
> *"Upon the signing of this MOU, the Islamic Republic of Iran will make arrangements using its best efforts for the safe passage of commercial vessels with no charge for 60 days, only from the Persian Gulf to the Sea of Oman and vice versa. The traffic of commercial vessels will immediately start, and considering the need for removing the technical and military obstacles and de-mining by the Islamic Republic of Iran will be instated within 30 days. The Islamic Republic of Iran will conduct dialog with the Sultanate of Oman to define the future administration and maritime services in the Strait of Hormuz, in discussion with other Persian Gulf littoral states in line with the applicable international law and the sovereign rights of coastal states of the Strait of Hormuz."*

---

### **IV. Did Iran Violate the MOU?**

**Technically, yes. Strategically, Iran disputes it.**

#### **The Case Against Iran:**
1. **Article 5 commits to "no charge" safe passage.** Firing missiles at LNG and crude tankers is the **opposite** of safe passage.
2. **"Best efforts" does not permit military enforcement of unilateral routing rules.** The MOU requires Iran to **consult Oman** on future administration—not impose routes by missile strike.
3. **The IRGC warning to ships** to use Iranian-designated corridors effectively created a **de facto blockade** of internationally recognized lanes.
4. **CENTCOM explicitly called the attacks** "unwarranted, dangerous, and a **clear violation of the ceasefire.**"

#### **Iran's Counterargument:**
1. **"Best efforts" is not an absolute guarantee.** Iran claims it was *safeguarding* the strait by preventing unauthorized transit.
2. **The vessels violated Iranian sovereign routing.** Tehran asserts the right to regulate traffic in its territorial waters.
3. **The U.S. failed to fully remove the blockade within 30 days.** Iran claims the U.S. violated **Article 4 first** by maintaining military presence and enforcement.
4. **The U.S. revoked oil sanctions relief—a breach of commitment.** Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned the Treasury Department's revocation of the sanctions waiver as a **"major violation."**

---

### **V. The U.S. Response: Airstrikes and Revoked Waivers**

The U.S. military response was described by officials as **larger than previous retaliatory strikes** against Iran.

#### **USCENTCOM Strikes (Tuesday, July 7):**
- Iranian **air-defense systems**
- **Coastal surveillance sites**
- **Surface-to-air missile (SAM) batteries**
- **Anti-ship cruise missile sites**
- **Drone launch sites**
- **Port facilities**

Strikes were delivered by **Air Force jets and Navy Tactical aircraft**. **President Trump approved the strike plan from Turkey**, where he was attending the NATO summit.

#### **Treasury Department Action:**
Simultaneously, the U.S. **revoked the temporary sanctions waiver** that had permitted Iran to sell oil and petrochemicals. The waiver—originally scheduled through **August 21**—was cut short. All authorized transactions must now wind down by **July 17**.

---

### **VI. The Funeral Context: Was the U.S. Supposed to Hold Fire?**

This is where the timeline becomes politically charged.

**Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was killed on February 28, 2026**, in the opening U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran. His body lay in state for nearly four months before the **state funeral began Saturday, July 4**. The ceremonies are ongoing and scheduled to conclude with burial in Mashhad on **July 9**.

#### **What Trump Said:**
> *"We gave them a week off for a funeral because we're nice."*
> — **Trump, July 4 speech at Mount Rushmore**

And to Axios:
> *"They are all there. One shot [and we can take them all out], but we are not going to do that because then we would have nobody to negotiate with."*

#### **The Reality:**
1. **There was no formal "no-hostilities" agreement** tied to the funeral. The pause was a **bilateral understanding** mediated by Qatar and Pakistan that peace talks would be temporarily suspended—not that military operations would cease.
2. **Trump explicitly framed the restraint as tactical, not principled.** He held fire on Iranian leadership gatherings for **strategic leverage** (needing negotiators alive), not out of obligation.
3. **However, the symbolism matters.** Launching strikes against Iran **during active mourning for the Supreme Leader killed by U.S. weapons** is a significant escalation of tone, even if not a formal treaty violation.
4. **Iran is weaponizing this narrative.** Iranian state media and the Foreign Ministry have used the funeral-period strikes to portray the U.S. as **duplicitous and fundamentally untrustworthy**—further eroding what little confidence existed in the MOU.

---

### **VII. The MOU Is Functionally Dead**

Let us be direct about what the current cycle reveals:

| MOU Commitment | What Actually Happened |
|----------------|------------------------|
| "Immediate permanent cessation of hostilities on all fronts" | Proxy tensions in Lebanon continued; IRGC struck commercial vessels |
| U.S. removes naval blockade within 30 days | Reduced but not fully lifted; enforcement mechanisms remained |
| Iran ensures "safe passage with no charge" for 60 days | Iran imposed route restrictions; attacked vessels using Omani corridor |
| 60-day negotiation window for final deal | Stalled before funeral; talks officially paused; military strikes ongoing |
| U.S. grants temporary sanctions relief | **Revoked** on July 7 in response to tanker attacks |

Both sides are treating the MOU as **transactional leverage**, not a binding framework. Each violation justifies escalation in a classic **security dilemma spiral**. The document was always fragile—it assumed good faith that neither party has demonstrated.

---

### **VIII. The Deeper Problem: Competing Interpretations**

As **Al Jazeera reported**:
> *"Tehran claims the memorandum is specifically worded to leave it, in consultation with Oman, to manage the reopening of the strait... At a briefing in Tehran, foreign ministry officials insisted Iran had a right to impose fees on all ships using the strait."*

But **Hassan Ahmadian**, University of Tehran:
> *"Washington is going back on the deal it signed on June 15. What we see is the United States trying to find its way out of this memorandum of understanding while obliging Iran to its end of the terms."*

**The core dispute:**
- **The U.S. reads Article 5 as requiring Iran to get out of the way** and let international shipping resume normally.
- **Iran reads Article 5 as confirming its role as gatekeeper** with sovereign rights to manage, restrict, and potentially charge for transit.

The MOU was **ambiguous by design**— drafted in haste to stop a war, with enough fuzziness for each side to claim compatibility with its national interests. But ambiguity in ceasefires does not produce peace. It produces **justifications for the next round of strikes.**

---

### **IX. What This Says About US Reliability**

The revocation of the sanctions waiver is the most telling detail.

The U.S. did not merely retaliate militarily for the tanker attacks. It **rolled back economic relief** that Iran viewed as central to the MOU bargain. From Tehran's perspective, this confirms a **bait-and-switch pattern**: extract Iranian restraint, then withhold promised benefits.

Trump's **"week off because we're nice"** framing reveals the fundamental attitude. The pause was **tactical courtesy**, not strategic commitment. The U.S. never intended to suspend military pressure during the funeral—it simply refrained from assassinating Iranian leadership gathered in public.

**This does not mean Iran is innocent.** Firing missiles at Qatari and Saudi energy tankers is an act of state-sponsored maritime piracy, no matter the routing dispute. But it does mean the U.S. approach—**military retaliation paired with economic punishment during active mourning**—all but guarantees the MOU collapses rather than matures into a final agreement.

### **X. The Stakes Going Forward**

| Scenario | Likelihood |
|----------|------------|
| Negotiations resume after Khamenei burial (July 9) | Possible but dimmed; Iranian leadership feels humiliated |
| Tit-for-tat escalation cycle continues | High; both sides now have "justification" for further strikes |
| Full war reignition | Contained for now; neither side wants full cost, but neither is de-escalating |
| Gulf states develop independent Hormuz security architecture | Increasing; UAE proposing IMO-based alternatives; Iran rejecting them |
| MOU formally declared void | Probable; both sides have materially breached core obligations |

The **60-day negotiation window** expires in mid-August. It is difficult to imagine a comprehensive nuclear and security deal emerging from talks that have been consumed by funerals, airstrikes, revoked sanctions relief, and ship-burning in the world's most critical oil chokepoint.

The MOU was supposed to build trust. Instead, it demonstrated **why trust was absent in the first place.**
